# MOS-5 — Run the first MOSAIC reader-comprehension test

- Source: https://linear.app/sheridanandbairns/issue/MOS-5/run-the-first-mosaic-reader-comprehension-test
- Status: Todo
- Project: Make the MOSAIC proposition clear and convincing
- Updated: 2026-09-24T17:11:21.631Z

## Outcome

Obtain direct evidence of whether first-time readers understand what MOSAIC is, when it is useful, what it produces, and how a worked example demonstrates its principles.

## Why

The current assessment is expert review rather than evidence from real readers. Broad copy or example changes made before observing readers could optimise the wrong problem.

## Scope

* Recruit approximately three people who are unfamiliar with MOSAIC and who make professional decisions with incomplete information.
* Run an unaided first-impression test of the opening proposition.
* Allow five minutes of free exploration, then ask the participant to explain MOSAIC without looking at the site.
* Use one paired worked example: ask the participant to predict what a change will do, observe the result, explain the principle, and transfer it to another situation.
* Ask whether MOSAIC is making the decision or supporting human judgement, how it differs from merely gathering more data, and what should happen when a critical gap is discovered.
* After the unaided baseline, test the candidate outcome-first proposition and questions about the first useful action and tangible result.
* Retain anonymised verbatim answers, navigation choices, hesitation, unfamiliar terms and confident misunderstandings.

## Constraints

* Do not explain MOSAIC before the unaided test.
* Do not require exact recall of the six stage names.
* Do not rewrite the proposition between participants in the initial round.
* Treat comprehension as demonstrated only when the participant explains causality or transfers a principle.
* Separate observed evidence from interpretation and recommendations.

## Verification

* Anonymised notes are added to the existing reader-comprehension project document.
* The evidence shows whether readers can identify the decision context, incomplete evidence, alternatives or uncertainty, justified action, tangible decision record and conditions for reconsideration.
* Findings support an explicit next decision about proposition copy, the complete worked example, or the first useful interaction.
