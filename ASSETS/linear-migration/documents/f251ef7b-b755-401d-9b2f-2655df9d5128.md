## Overall assessment

The substance appears better than the packaging. Beneath the scarcity and "million-dollar secret" framing is a coherent playbook for moving from attention to customers and then to a repeatable sales system.

The underlying sequence is:

**Get attention → make the message resonate → obtain a small commitment → make a clear ask → make the economics work → orchestrate a sustained campaign**

The video also appears to use the techniques while explaining them: scarcity, exclusivity, a free giveaway, repeated calls to action, micro-commitments, customer-centred benefits, a large prize, and the book launch woven throughout. The tutorial is itself part of the funnel.

## The 6M framework

The supplied assessment describes six elements. The first "M" was not explicitly named in the supplied transcript, so its label below is descriptive rather than canonical.

### 1. Attract attention with something people value

Mechanisms described include:

* exclusivity;
* direct self-reference to a recognisable person or problem;
* reciprocity through useful value before an ask;
* concrete, specific giveaways rather than vague "free training";
* delivering value before the desired action, rather than disguising a transaction.

General principle:

> Do not merely promise value. Demonstrate some of the value before asking for commitment.

#### MOSAIC implication

MOSAIC should let people experience useful reasoning before asking them to adopt a framework, start a commercial conversation, or trust a larger product claim. A genuinely useful example or small tool can be both value and explanation.

### 2. Message: the customer is Luke; MOSAIC is Yoda

The customer is the protagonist. The product, company, or framework is the mechanism or guide.

Rule:

**Ideal state first → mechanism second**

Lead with the change a person wants, then explain how MOSAIC helps produce it. Do not lead with structured evidence, knowledge graphs, uncertainty models, AI architecture, or decision traceability simply because those mechanisms are technically interesting.

Candidate outcome-led language:

> **Make important decisions you can understand, explain and defend.**

#### MOSAIC implication

This is directly relevant to the active project **Make the MOSAIC proposition clear and convincing**. The proposition should first communicate the reader's desired outcome and recognisable decision problem. The framework and six stages can then explain the mechanism.

This is a hypothesis to test with readers, not approved replacement copy.

### 3. Micro-commitment: get the first valuable yes

Do not ask a stranger immediately for the largest commitment. Find the easiest valuable action that reduces friction and teaches something about genuine intent.

The source journey was described as:

**free registration → small paid upgrade → larger commercial relationship**

A possible MOSAIC journey is:

**read an article → try a free tool → analyse one decision → save or share the result → use MOSAIC seriously**

#### MOSAIC implication

The current website should not jump conceptually from "interesting framework" to enterprise-scale adoption. It should reveal a useful, proportionate next step.

The strategically important question is:

> What is the easiest valuable yes somebody can give MOSAIC?

Possible answers require validation. They might include trying one worked example, framing one decision, or receiving a useful decision record.

### 4. Make the ask: clarity beats brevity

Very short calls to action such as "Learn more", "Click here", or "Subscribe" may conceal rather than reduce friction.

A good call to action should clarify:

* why the reader should care;
* what they receive;
* what action to take;
* how large the commitment is;
* why the exchange is worthwhile.

Additional principles from the supplied assessment:

* use one primary call to action consistently;
* use a strong, explicit verb;
* make the benefit personal;
* keep the ask proportionate to the relationship;
* frame it as an exchange of value rather than a favour to the organisation.

General principle:

> **Clarity beats brevity.**

#### MOSAIC implication

The proposition project should assess whether each primary call to action describes a meaningful exchange. "Explore the framework" and "Try a worked example" are clear actions, but the reader may still need to know what useful result they will obtain.

CTA testing should follow proposition comprehension, not substitute for it.

### 5. Money: own the customer relationship

The broader principle is that revenue alone does not make a good business. Protect margin, understand acquisition economics, and retain a direct relationship with the audience or customer.

The supplied account emphasises:

* direct communication;
* customer data and distribution control;
* gross margin relative to customer-acquisition cost;
* turning a launch into reusable evergreen material rather than accepting a single spike followed by collapse.

Sequence:

**launch → acquire audience → make an offer → learn what works → turn it into evergreen material → continue**

#### MOSAIC implication

This is more relevant to future adoption and sustainability than to the immediate proposition work. MOSAIC should eventually avoid dependence on channels that obscure who is receiving value and why. However, detailed unit economics would be premature before a validated useful offer and evidence of actual demand.

### 6. Map: orchestrate a campaign

A launch is described as a coordinated sequence of exposures rather than one announcement. Frequency and explicitness increase over time while visual motifs, stories, examples, and ordinary content create familiarity.

General principle:

> A launch is not a post. It is a sustained, coherent sequence that gradually makes the idea familiar.

#### MOSAIC implication

When MOSAIC has something sufficiently useful to launch, promotion should combine essays, examples, demonstrations, tools, and repeated ideas into a coherent world. Grounds to Act, decision records, fragmented evidence, and defensible action already provide potentially distinctive narrative material.

The language of subliminal triggers, artificial scarcity, disappearing content, and activation colours is not something MOSAIC should copy. It conflicts with the existing principles of trust, explicit reasoning, and no dark patterns.

## What is worth retaining

Stripped of the theatrical packaging:

1. Give people something genuinely useful before demanding anything.
2. Talk about the customer's desired outcome, not primarily about the organisation or technology.
3. Design a ladder of increasingly meaningful commitments.
4. Make the next action unmistakably clear and explain its value.
5. Own the audience relationship and understand the economics.
6. Treat promotion as a sustained, coherent campaign rather than one announcement.

## Relevance to MOSAIC now

The most immediately relevant ideas are **Message, Micro-commitment, and Make the ask**.

They bear directly on the current gap between:

> This is an interesting framework.

and:

> I understand why and when I would use this, what I will get, and what I should do next.

They should be treated as hypotheses for the active proposition and reader-comprehension work:

* Can readers state the desired outcome before they know the mechanics?
* Can they identify a useful first action?
* Do they understand what they receive by taking it?
* Does the next step feel proportionate to their current trust and interest?

## Relationship to the MOSAIC Mandala

This assessment intersects four Mandala conditions:

* **Demonstrated value:** give useful value before asking for commitment.
* **Compelling product:** create a meaningful first experience and visible result.
* **Reach & market:** sharpen positioning, free tools, essays, and coherent campaigns.
* **Adoption & sustainability:** create a commitment ladder and retain a direct learning relationship with users.

It reinforces the Mandala hypothesis:

> **Formalise it → demonstrate it → put it in people's hands.**

It does not by itself justify a new project.

## Guardrails

* Do not imitate manipulative scarcity or hype.
* Do not treat marketing claims as verified evidence without the original source.
* Do not optimise calls to action before the proposition is understandable.
* Do not construct a commercial funnel before there is a genuinely useful first experience.
* Do not turn the six ideas into six issues automatically.
* Preserve human agency and make the exchange explicit.

## Possible later decisions

These are questions for planning, not committed tasks:

1. Test outcome-first proposition language against the current wording.
2. Define the easiest valuable first "yes" for MOSAIC.
3. Decide what tangible result a first interaction should produce.
4. Test whether the primary CTA makes that exchange clear.
5. Later, decide whether a sustained content and demonstration campaign is justified by a validated offer.

## Planning decision — 17 September 2026

### Adopt now as test principles

* Lead with the reader’s desired outcome and recognisable decision problem; explain MOSAIC as the mechanism afterwards.
* Give a person something genuinely useful before asking for greater commitment.
* Make the value exchange and tangible result explicit.
* Preserve human agency, trust and transparent reasoning.
* Treat specific proposition wording as a hypothesis to test, not approved replacement copy.
* Establish proposition comprehension before optimising calls to action.

The initial reader test should therefore examine whether a first-time reader can identify the desired outcome, when MOSAIC is useful, what tangible result it produces, what useful first action is available, and whether that action feels proportionate to their current understanding and trust.

### Retain as open questions

The easiest valuable first “yes” has not yet been selected. Candidates include trying a worked example, framing one real decision, or receiving a useful decision record. Reader evidence should inform that choice.

A commitment ladder, direct audience relationship, free tool, reusable example packs, acquisition economics and a sustained content or launch campaign remain potentially valuable later. They require a validated useful first experience and evidence of demand.

### Defer

Defer CTA optimisation, funnel design, audience ownership, campaign orchestration, unit economics and launch planning until readers understand MOSAIC and a useful first interaction has been validated.

### Reject

Reject artificial scarcity, hype, subliminal framing, disappearing-content tactics and other dark patterns. Do not treat the uncaptured video’s quantitative claims as evidence. The source claims remain unverified; source verification is unnecessary for the current plan because these ideas are being treated only as hypotheses for testing.

This assessment does not justify a new project or six implementation issues.
