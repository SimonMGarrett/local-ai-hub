## Status and authority

This document assesses planning material in the Obsidian area `003 - SHERIDANandBAIRNS`. The source notes pre-date MOSAIC.

They are historical inputs, not MOSAIC requirements, decisions or backlog. Current MOSAIC decisions, terminology, methodology, Linear planning, and implemented behaviour take precedence whenever there is a conflict.

The current authoritative planning context is the **MOSAIC Adoption** initiative, the **MOSAIC Mandala — strategic map and next-phase hypothesis**, and the active project **Make the MOSAIC proposition clear and convincing**.

## Why retain this assessment

The historical material contains ideas that independently converge with parts of current MOSAIC thinking. Those ideas may strengthen later reasoning, positioning or commercial exploration, but must pass through current MOSAIC evidence and decision processes before becoming commitments.

## Compatible historical threads

### Decisions under costly uncertainty

The clearest recurring historical proposition is helping organisations make sense of complex, risky and fragmented information where blind spots are expensive.

This is compatible with MOSAIC's current centre:

> MOSAIC becomes a credible, useful and adopted system for making defensible decisions under uncertainty.

The historical phrasing is useful as candidate audience language, not approved MOSAIC copy.

### Decision support, not decision ownership

The old business-planning material distinguishes analysis and decision support from legal, compliance or operational sign-off. It argues that the client or human decision-maker retains responsibility.

This reinforces current MOSAIC commitments to human agency, explicit judgement and defensibility. It does not add a new requirement.

### Epistemic discipline

The historical notes repeatedly emphasise:

* what is known and unknown;
* incomplete data and blind spots;
* confidence and uncertainty signalling;
* alternative explanations;
* explicit scope and intended use;
* language that avoids claiming certainty.

These themes are already represented more rigorously in the current Mandala under **Evidence & knowledge**, **Uncertainty & reasoning**, and **Trust & defensibility**. The current MOSAIC formulation is authoritative.

### Messy inputs

Several old notes assume users begin with disconnected spreadsheets, documents, emails or datasets rather than a clean model.

This supports the existing future MOSAIC candidate of accepting messy and unstructured inputs while retaining provenance and traceability. It does not justify implementation work during the current cycle.

### Real-user validation

The historical company plans repeatedly say that ideas should meet real people before substantial execution, and suggest a small number of deep conversations with aligned organisations.

This is consistent with the active reader-comprehension work and the Mandala's later adoption hypotheses. Current reader evidence should come first; the legacy material does not justify a new commercial-validation project now.

### Candidate audiences

Historical audience hypotheses include people responsible for risk, compliance, intelligence or insight, strategy, national infrastructure and other settings where uncertainty is consequential.

These remain unvalidated candidates. MOSAIC should not adopt them as its target market without current evidence.

### Safe future engagement

If MOSAIC later supports a commercial service or pilot, the old notes contain useful reminders to define:

* the question being addressed;
* included and excluded evidence;
* intended use of outputs;
* known limitations and uncertainty;
* what remains a human, legal, compliance or operational judgement.

These are future design inputs, not legal guidance and not committed work.

## Historical ideas not promoted into MOSAIC

The following remain outside the current MOSAIC plan:

* particular consultancy packages or prices;
* data-cleaning, knowledge-graph, private-AI and OSINT product concepts;
* lead magnets, funnels and campaign tactics;
* proposed organisation and hiring structures;
* website aesthetics and legacy Sheridan & Bairns service language;
* unverified market-size estimates;
* legacy project codenames and product ideas.

They may still matter to Sheridan & Bairns independently, but must not be mistaken for MOSAIC strategy.

## Effect on current planning

This review creates no new initiative, project or issue.

It does not change Cycle 1. The current focus remains:

1. obtain evidence from first-time readers;
2. represent tied explanations honestly in the Structure + Analysis example;
3. use the resulting evidence to decide whether to change the proposition, the complete-decision example, or the first useful interaction.

The historical material should be revisited only when a current MOSAIC decision makes one of its hypotheses relevant.
