# MOSAIC Mandala

## Status and intent

This strategic map was developed by applying Mandala goal setting to MOSAIC and captured on 16 September 2026. It is a hypothesis for later review, not an agreed roadmap.

The eight inner boxes are conditions for success, not departments or arbitrary categories. The 64 second-level cells are the map, not the backlog.

## Centre

**MOSAIC becomes a credible, useful and adopted system for making defensible decisions under uncertainty.**

## First ring: eight conditions for success

1. Rigorous methodology
2. Evidence & knowledge
3. Uncertainty & reasoning
4. Compelling product
5. Demonstrated value
6. Trust & defensibility
7. Reach & market
8. Adoption & sustainability

Each is a potential failure condition: MOSAIC could have excellent software without proof of value, a strong methodology without reach, interest without a trustworthy audit trail, or demonstrations without sustained adoption.

## 1. Rigorous methodology

Aim: make MOSAIC more than a collection of sensible prompts.

* Define exactly what a decision is
* Formalise the six-stage process
* Define stage entry and exit criteria
* Separate evidence, inference and judgement
* Model assumptions explicitly
* Define how decisions are revised
* Document failure modes
* Publish the underlying framework

**Potential intellectual signature:** evidence is not inference, and inference is not judgement.

## 2. Evidence & knowledge

This corresponds closely to the analytical layer.

* Capture provenance
* Represent evidence relationships
* Accept messy and unstructured inputs
* Identify missing evidence
* Detect contradictions
* Represent source characteristics
* Support knowledge graphs
* Preserve traceability to originals

MOSAIC should not require perfect input. It should be good at saying:

> Here is what you actually know, here is why you think you know it, and here is what you do not know.

## 3. Uncertainty & reasoning

Uncertainty should be a first-class pillar rather than disappear inside the evidence model.

* Represent multiple uncertainty types
* Avoid false numerical precision
* Handle conflicting evidence
* Expose assumptions
* Compare plausible interpretations
* Model evidence dimensions
* Show sensitivity to uncertainty
* Identify decision-changing unknowns

The evidence-space idea **E = (o, s, a, r, c, d)** belongs here.

A potentially defining question is:

> Which uncertainty, if resolved, has the greatest chance of changing the decision?

This turns uncertainty into something actionable rather than merely acknowledged.

## 4. Compelling product

The framework must become something people enjoy and value using.

* Excellent decision intake
* Guided evidence exploration
* Clear visual decision workspace
* Useful AI assistance
* Human remains in control
* Excellent reasoning trace
* Action hand-off
* Iterative decision review

Avoid making the first version an enormous enterprise application.

The intended magic moment is:

> I came in thinking the question was X. MOSAIC helped me realise the real question was Y, showed me what I was assuming, and revealed that Z was the thing I actually needed to find out.

## 5. Demonstrated value

This may be the most important near-term quadrant.

* Tiny understandable example
* Rich realistic example
* Messy-data example
* Before and after comparison
* Interactive website demonstration
* Real user case study
* Compare with normal LLM use
* Publish reusable example packs

Current hypothesis: MOSAIC needs proof through examples more than additional theory.

A strong set could span:

* Everyday: Should I move house?
* Professional: Should we adopt technology X?
* Analytical or intelligence: Given incomplete and conflicting evidence, what action is justified?

If the same framework visibly improves all three, MOSAIC becomes understandable as a general decision-management approach rather than another AI application.

## 6. Trust & defensibility

This is a substantial point of distinction from asking an LLM what to do.

* Every claim traceable
* Assumptions visible
* Human judgement identified
* Alternatives preserved
* Changes recorded
* Privacy and deployment options
* Reproducible reasoning
* Explicit limitations

Potential promise:

> MOSAIC does not merely tell you what decision was made. It preserves the grounds on which it was reasonable to make it.

This aligns with Grounds to Act.

A good outcome is not the same thing as a good decision. A well-made decision can produce a bad outcome because the world is uncertain. MOSAIC should preserve why the choice was reasonable given the evidence available at the time.

## 7. Reach & market

People need to discover the idea and immediately understand why they care.

* Sharp positioning
* Excellent website
* Publish distinctive essays
* Useful free tools
* Talks and demonstrations
* Build a practitioner community
* Reach specific professions
* Partnerships and integrations

A useful standalone tool may teach MOSAIC more effectively than an invitation to read about the framework.

Candidate: **Decision Framer**. A user enters a question such as "Should we replace system X?" and receives a structured decision definition, assumptions, stakeholders, uncertainties, and missing evidence. It should be useful independently while teaching how MOSAIC thinks.

## 8. Adoption & sustainability

MOSAIC must survive contact with real organisations and real people.

* Identify strongest initial users
* Conduct user interviews
* Run real pilots
* Make onboarding easy
* Define value and success measures
* Establish a commercial model
* Learn from decision outcomes
* Feed learning back into MOSAIC

The eventual learning loop is:

**Evidence → Decision → Action → Outcome → Learning → Better future decisions**

This would make MOSAIC a decision-learning system rather than a static framework.

## The map is not the backlog

Do not create 64 tasks.

Eight high-leverage candidates for later evaluation are:

1. Formalise exactly what MOSAIC means by a decision.
2. Separate evidence, inference, assumption and judgement.
3. Define the decision-changing unknowns concept.
4. Create one exceptionally good realistic worked example.
5. Build one small free MOSAIC tool.
6. Demonstrate complete traceability from decision back to evidence.
7. Sharpen the one-sentence explanation of MOSAIC.
8. Put MOSAIC in front of real users and observe what happens.

The next phase may reduce to three movements:

> **Formalise it → demonstrate it → put it in people's hands.**

The working diagnosis is that additional conceptual exploration may now have diminishing returns. One excellent worked decision and a small usable tool may reveal more about what MOSAIC needs next than further framework writing.

## Questions for review

* Are these eight conditions genuinely necessary and collectively sufficient?
* Where is MOSAIC currently most unbalanced?
* Which one to three outcomes would generate the most learning now?
* Which candidates belong inside existing projects?
* Does any candidate justify a new project?
* What should explicitly remain uncommitted?

Do not translate the Mandala into projects or issues until those decisions are made.

## Planning decision — 17 September 2026

### Status of the map

The centre and eight conditions are accepted as a useful working architecture for planning, not as a proven or final decomposition. The Mandala remains strategic context rather than a roadmap or backlog.

### Current diagnosis

**Demonstrated Value is the clearest present imbalance.** MOSAIC already has substantial conceptual material, but there is no evidence yet from real first-time readers and parts of the worked examples do not fully demonstrate the principles they claim to teach.

Methodological integrity, trust and defensibility remain constraints on every demonstration. In particular, MOSAIC must keep evidence, inference, assumptions and judgement distinguishable; avoid false numerical precision; preserve human agency; and show what information could change a decision.

### Cycle 1 outcomes

1. Put the current proposition and a worked example in front of first-time readers and retain evidence of comprehension, misunderstanding and transfer.
2. Correct the Structure + Analysis example so tied explanations are represented honestly rather than declaring an arbitrary analytical lead.

Both outcomes belong in the existing project **Make the MOSAIC proposition clear and convincing**. No new project is justified.

### Retained future outcomes

The following remain valuable strategic candidates, but are not committed Cycle 1 work:

* formalise MOSAIC’s definition of a decision;
* sharpen the separation of evidence, inference, assumption and judgement;
* define and test the decision-changing unknowns concept;
* create one exceptionally strong complete worked decision;
* demonstrate end-to-end traceability from evidence to decision;
* determine the most useful first interaction and tangible output;
* consider a small Decision Framer only if evidence supports it as that first interaction;
* expand testing across everyday, professional and analytical contexts;
* eventually close the evidence-to-outcome learning loop.

The next likely decision is whether reader evidence warrants proposition changes, a redesign of the complete-decision example, or a different first interaction.

### Explicit non-commitments

Do not create 64 issues, build an enterprise-scale product, create a new project, or commit the free-tool, campaign, community, partnership, commercial-model or broad framework-formalisation work merely because it appears in the map.
