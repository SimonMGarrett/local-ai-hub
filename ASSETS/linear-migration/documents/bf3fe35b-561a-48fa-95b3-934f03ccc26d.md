## Status and authority

This register captures possible use cases, audiences and adoption ideas found in pre-MOSAIC Sheridan & Bairns notes.

They are historical hypotheses, not approved MOSAIC positioning, target markets or backlog. Current MOSAIC evidence and Linear decisions take precedence.

## Candidate decision contexts

### Organisational crisis and resilience

The CyNam material describes decisions made during cyber disruption across people, process, technology and communications.

This could eventually be a strong MOSAIC context because evidence is incomplete, time matters, interpretations compete and actions have consequences. The event description itself is not evidence that MOSAIC fits the problem.

### Investigations and fragmented evidence

Multiple old product ideas involve people, companies, watchlists, ownership, sanctions, supply chains, news, social material and relationship analysis.

The MOSAIC-relevant part is not the specific OSINT product. It is the decision pattern:

* heterogeneous evidence;
* uncertain identity and provenance;
* missing or dependent sources;
* competing explanations;
* a human decision that must remain justified and revisable.

### Technology and supplier choices

The old notes contain many tool and architecture comparisons. A bounded professional decision such as whether to adopt a technology may be useful for demonstrating MOSAIC, provided the example preserves evidence quality, constraints, alternatives, uncertainty and reconsideration triggers.

The tools themselves are not MOSAIC requirements.

## Criteria for promoting an old idea into a MOSAIC example

A historical scenario should only become MOSAIC work when it provides:

1. a real human decision rather than only detection or analysis;
2. meaningful alternatives;
3. incomplete, conflicting or dependent evidence;
4. consequences and constraints that affect the choice;
5. assumptions and unknowns worth exposing;
6. a justified action and a condition for reconsideration;
7. material that can be shared safely and understood by the intended audience.

## Adoption ideas worth retaining as hypotheses

The old material repeatedly recommends:

* test ideas with a small number of real people before building heavily;
* explain value and desired outcomes before technical mechanism;
* give people a useful first experience;
* use clear, proportionate next steps;
* learn through deep relationships rather than broad undifferentiated outreach;
* turn repeated, evidenced needs into reusable products or services.

These themes are already represented more carefully in the current MOSAIC Mandala, the Codie Sanchez assessment and the reader-comprehension project. They do not create new work.

## Material not promoted

This review does not adopt:

* old ideal-client profiles as MOSAIC's target market;
* historic contacts or meeting notes as outreach lists;
* high-ticket consultancy pricing or offer structures;
* lead-magnet, funnel or scarcity tactics;
* a broad launch or content campaign;
* Sheridan & Bairns website aesthetics or old service language;
* career plans, hiring models or project codenames.

## Effect on current planning

No new initiative, project or issue is justified. The near-term priority remains evidence from first-time readers and honest worked examples.

After that evidence is reviewed, this register may help select participants or a realistic next worked decision.
