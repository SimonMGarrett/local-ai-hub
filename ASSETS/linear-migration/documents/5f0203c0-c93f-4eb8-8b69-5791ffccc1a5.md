## Potentially useful technical threads

### Accept messy inputs without pretending they are clean

Several notes assume that useful work begins with inconsistent files, mixed formats, missing values and disconnected sources. They explore conversion, schema discovery, profiling, normalisation, sampling and data-quality checks.

This supports the current MOSAIC hypothesis that evidence intake should tolerate messy and unstructured material while preserving what was supplied. It does not imply that MOSAIC should become a general ETL platform.

### Separate conversion, understanding and interpretation

The Quirky Gerbil material distinguishes converting data into a usable form from understanding its structure and then presenting it meaningfully.

That separation is compatible with MOSAIC's stronger distinction between evidence, inference and judgement. The old component names and designs should not be adopted directly.

### Preserve relationships, identity and provenance

Historical work on entity extraction, record linkage, graphs and source enrichment may later help MOSAIC represent:

* when records refer to the same entity;
* relationships between evidence items;
* source dependence and duplication;
* traceability to original material;
* context gathered from additional sources.

### Make uncertainty and model behaviour inspectable

The anomaly-detection, HMM and analytical notes favour explicit features, transparent scoring and visible uncertainty rather than unexplained conclusions.

That instinct is compatible with MOSAIC, but the specific statistical methods belong to particular analytical use cases, not the core MOSAIC method.

Sources include:

* `003 - PROJECT DEVELOPMENT/AIS HMM.md`
* `700 - RESEARCH/_OUTLINE.md`
* `004 - TECH and DATA/Monte Carlo Models.md`

### Prefer observable behaviour over declared design

The note `Understanding Complex Systems.md` suggests examining actual exchanges between system components rather than trusting specifications alone.

For MOSAIC, this may be useful as a general evidence principle: distinguish claimed behaviour from observed behaviour. It is a candidate insight, not an adopted rule.

### Visualisation should support reasoning, not merely presentation

The older material repeatedly treats visualisation as a way to reveal patterns, relationships and context. A MOSAIC interface should only adopt these ideas where they help users inspect evidence, alternatives, uncertainty and decision consequences.

The old dashboard, Streamlit, Power BI and graph-interface choices are not current product decisions.

### Local, offline and constrained deployment

Notes on local language models, air-gapped ClickHouse and private tooling show an enduring concern for restricted environments.

This is compatible with the Mandala's future privacy and deployment-options thread. It does not justify current infrastructure work.

The archived Trivy article and security notes reinforce that a trustworthy decision system depends on its software supply chain and operational environment. This belongs in future threat modelling and deployment assurance, not the current proposition cycle.

## Ideas explicitly not promoted

This review does not select:

* ClickHouse, DuckDB, NocoDB, Mage, Streamlit, Power BI or any other named stack;
* a lakehouse or knowledge graph as mandatory architecture;
* blockchain as a proof-of-deletion mechanism;
* a general analyst operating system;
* a generic data-conversion product;
* AIS anomaly detection as core MOSAIC functionality;
* autonomous source gathering or web scraping;
* a particular scoring, clustering or entity-resolution technique.
