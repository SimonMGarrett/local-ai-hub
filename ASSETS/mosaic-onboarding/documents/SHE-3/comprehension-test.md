## Explain-back before quiz
The four-dimension rubric and 4/4 cutoff are this study’s proposed comprehension criteria, not a canonical MOSAIC assessment.
Allow three minutes. Exact prompt: “In your own words, what is MOSAIC, who is responsible for the decision, and how would it help someone move from incomplete evidence to a choice?” Only follow-up: “Anything else?” No stage-name cues.
Two raters independently score verbatim responses, blind to quiz scores. Each dimension is 0 or 1; no partial points:
1. Purpose: a framework connecting an explainable choice to incomplete/conflicting/scattered evidence. “Helps decisions” alone is insufficient.
2. Process: connects framing the decision, examining material/sources/assumptions and alternatives, and considering consequences before a reasoned choice. Stage names alone are insufficient; exact terminology unnecessary.
3. Revisability: describes returning to reasoning when gaps emerge and/or explicit conditions for reconsidering a choice. A rigid six-step account alone scores zero.
4. Ownership/limits: a human owns the choice and uncertainty remains. Automatic answers or guaranteed outcomes score zero.
Explain-back pass = **4/4**. Keep supporting excerpts, both initial scores and reconciliation rationale. Resolve differences using these anchors; unresolved dimensions score zero conservatively. Do not revise thresholds after results.

## Quiz — participant sheet
Five minutes, page closed. Select one answer per question. Each correct answer = 1; incorrect, blank or multiple = 0; no partial credit. Reveal answers only after both assessments.

1. What is MOSAIC primarily for?
A. Storing all information.
B. Helping a person connect a choice to evidence, assumptions and reasons.
C. Predicting outcomes with certainty.
D. Replacing the decision owner.

2. You have lots of data but no clear decision. What should you clarify first?
A. One score for the data.
B. The most popular explanation.
C. The decision, owner, options and constraints.
D. Report design.

3. Two reports repeat one original observation. How should they be handled?
A. Keep the shared origin visible rather than assuming independent support.
B. Count two independent confirmations.
C. Delete both because repeated evidence is useless.
D. Average conclusions and remove sources.

4. Analysis exposes an assumption that could change the choice. What fits MOSAIC?
A. Ignore it after Analysis finishes.
B. Always collect all possible information.
C. Automatically pick the safest-sounding option.
D. Revisit relevant reasoning and consider whether targeted inquiry is worthwhile.

5. Which record supports later review?
A. Only the option and date.
B. Reasons, alternatives, remaining uncertainty and reconsideration conditions.
C. A score without sources.
D. Only whether the eventual outcome was good.

## Analysis rules and empty register
For each P01–P10 retain exposure time/deviations, four rubric scores, explain-back pass, five responses, quiz total, quiz ≥4 and **joint pass = explain-back pass AND quiz ≥4**.
Target met only if joint passes ≥8 among these same ten. Separate counts of eight explainers and eight quiz passes do not prove this intersection. Report individual anonymized rows, X/10 joint passes and deviations.
Incomplete assessments are not passes. Do not replace poor results. Respect withdrawal/erasure and report missing data; do not claim a complete ten-person result. Repeat technically invalid sessions only for documented performance-independent reasons, retaining prior exposure information. Major exposure failures require a fresh frozen run rather than selective exclusion.
After scoring, ask what was confusing for future revisions; do not use feedback to alter this run's scores. A revised page needs a newly authorized cohort. Do not pool revisions or generalize a small-sample success into population validation.
P01–P10: not recruited, all results absent. Joint passes: **not measured**.
