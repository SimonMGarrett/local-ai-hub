## Plain-language account
Published principle: MOSAIC helps a decision owner move from incomplete or conflicting evidence to an explainable choice. It makes the decision context, sources, assumptions, alternatives, uncertainty and reasons inspectable. Its six forms of reasoning can be revisited as gaps emerge. It supports judgement; it does not promise certainty or transfer responsibility to an automated recommendation. [S3, S4]

The six names below are transcribed from the published framework, not invented from the acronym. Definitions are curator paraphrases checked against S4's purpose, questions and outputs.

| ID | Published form | Plain-language definition and expected result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Meaning | Specify the actual decision, its owner, available choices, desired outcome, timing, constraints and consequences, including reversibility and delay. Result: a decision statement. | S4, meaning |
| C2 | Observations | Inventory available material and distinguish direct observation, report, inference and assumption; retain origins and gaps. Result: evidence inventory with provenance and limitations. | S4, observations |
| C3 | Structure | Connect material to claims, alternatives and choices while preserving dependencies, contradictions, assumptions and uncertainty. Result: traceable relationships, not just a tidy summary. | S4, structure |
| C4 | Analysis | Examine patterns, corroboration, contradiction, sensitivity and competing explanations. Result: findings with assumptions, provenance, limits and unresolved questions. | S4, analysis |
| C5 | Interpretation | Determine how findings affect the consequences, risks and trade-offs of possible choices; identify uncertainty that could change the decision. Result: decision-relevant implications. | S4, interpretation |
| C6 | Choice | Commit to the course best supported in context, potentially including targeted inquiry when its value warrants its cost. Record rationale, rejected alternatives, residual uncertainty and reconsideration conditions. | S4, choice |

## Cross-cutting principles and boundaries
- C7: The forms are iterative; numbered stages do not require a one-way pipeline. A choice may justify inquiry; analysis may expose missing material. [S3, S4]
- C8: Preserve provenance and disagreement. Repeated reports from one origin must not silently become independent corroboration. [S4; supporting illustration S7]
- C9: Begin with the decision rather than whatever data happens to exist. Ask whether attainable information could change the choice; more information is not automatically better. [S3, S4]
- C10: Findings do not alone determine action. Consequences, constraints, values and reversibility matter. [S4; supporting illustration S8]
- C11: External analytical work supplies material to the decision layer; operational systems implement the choice. Handoff includes rationale, constraints, uncertainty and reconsideration conditions. MOSAIC also includes a form named Analysis within the decision layer. [S4]
- C12: Outcomes may later be linked to the decision record to learn which assumptions held. Preserve what was known at decision time; distinguish decision quality from outcome quality. [S4]
- C13: Human judgement and ownership remain. The website presents examples as educational simplifications. [S3, S9 and site's shared example disclaimer]

## Supporting evidence, inference and proposals
Supporting evidence here is evidence of published framework content. S6–S9 contain attempted illustrations of framing, provenance, consequences and assumption sensitivity. S10 cautions that none of the current teaching examples should be taken as entirely useful; ignore them or refer to them only loosely. None is a validation study or an observed success rate.

Inference I1: A decision record should be navigable from action back to claims and original material. This follows C3/C6/C8 and the home page's traceability instruction. This is a practical reading, not a prescribed database schema.
Inference I2: Evidence sufficiency is choice-relative: the same material may justify a small reversible step but not a major irreversible commitment. This follows S4 and the home page examples. No universal numerical threshold was located.
Proposal P1: Use a claim/source/assumption/alternative/choice register and versioned review log for company knowledge. This is the curator's implementation aid, not an added MOSAIC stage.
Proposal P2 — deferred after S10: refine examples illustrating the six stages. The existing synthetic example is retained as an unendorsed archival draft, not an official or recommended teaching application. No refinement task or testing is authorized by this record.

## Contradictions and material uncertainty
T1 — Scoring tension: S4's Structure failure mode cautions against compressing mixed evidence into one score. S7 counts signals and S9 uses weighted ordinal support. S10 confirms that these are teaching devices and no canonical scoring method is established here. Keep the examples and warning together; do not promote their arithmetic into MOSAIC doctrine.
T2 — Tie treatment: with all default S7 signals and shared provenance linked, each explanation receives one signal. The code still chooses the first sorted entry as the lead. This is a static implementation finding, not a browser test. The support counts alone do not justify preferring that explanation.
T3 — Two uses of Analysis: S4 distinguishes the analytical layer from the decision layer, while naming an internal form Analysis. Preserve both usages; S10 confirms the boundary: external analysis supplies material; MOSAIC Analysis examines structured evidence for this decision.
T4 — Status: S5 calls the framework developing. S10 now confirms C1–C13 as a bounded company baseline; the developing status and limits of this source set remain.
